LANES AND HANDOFF

#Your handoff counts in the new-patient path

1. Start, then “New patient?”: both in Front Desk. No handoff.
2. “Yes” to “pay for hospital card”: Front Desk to Patient. Handoff 1.
3. Pay to “Write patient details”: Patient to Front Desk. Handoff 2.
4. Write, then refer, then “Patient have a folder?”: all in Front Desk. None.
5. “No” to “create new folder”: Front Desk to Records. Handoff 3.
6. Create to “Carry folder”: both in Records. None.
7. Carry to “Wait in queue”: Records to Patient. Handoff 4.
8. Wait to “Consult patient”: Patient to Doctor. Handoff 5.
9. Consult to the end event: Doctor to Patient. Handoff 6.

a total of 6 counts. 

#Your handoff counts in the returning-patient path
1. Start, then “New patient?”: both in Front Desk. No handoff.
2. “No” to “verify returning patient”: No handoff.
3. After verified, refer patient to record: No handoff.
4. search for patient folder: front desk to record. Handoff 1.
5. After folder found, patient is sent to queue: Record to patient. Handoff 2.
6. Patient is then consulted by a doctor: Patient to doctor. Handoff 3
7. After consultation, Patient leaves with prescription: Doctor to patient. Handoff 4.

#DANGEROUS HANDOFF
For both returning and new patients, the dangerous handoff is handoff having folders move from record to consultation room. 

Handoff may add time, but not all the time added is the same. Take, for example, it is easier to just pick a new folder and fill it than to search for an existing folder in a very large pile. Based on the stated reason, returning patients tend to waste more time than new patients.
