Trigger1: A patient pays for a hospital card at the OPD front desk (for a new patient),
Trigger2: A returning patient is manually verified at the front desk and sent to Step 2.
Step 1: About 10 minutes for a new file, 15 or more for a returning patient (my assumption). The cashier writes the patient's name, age, and gender in a record book and on the clinic card.
Step 2: Assuming an average of 5 minutes walk to the record department. The cashier then refers the patient to the records department for file opening.
Step 3: About 15 minutes for a new file, 30 or more for a returning patient (my assumption). The records department then rewrites the patient's name on a flat folder. In case of a returning patient, the records clerk searches for the folder in a pile of other folders.
Step 4: The records clerk takes the folder to the doctor in the consulting room (assuming an average of another 5-minute walk).
Step 5: The patient waits outside the consultation room for their name to be called.
Result: The patient gets to be consulted by a doctor (assuming an interval of 15 minutes between consultations).
Pain points: Step 1, the patient’s name is written three times: the record book, the card, then the folder. Same data, three handwritten copies. This makes backups difficult and time-consuming, while manual verification for returning patients adds another layer of time-wasting.
Step 2  and step 3 in case of emergency mean a patient collapsing or heavy bleeding, resulting in further medical complications. Step 4 involves manual file handling, which raises data privacy and security concerns. Also, the file can be damaged or go missing. Step 5 was supposed to be the only understandable waste of time in the queue, but due to compounded time wasted in the previous steps, it becomes more frustrating for the patients, especially when there is no automatic queue numbering, which brings about the possibility of queue shunting.
